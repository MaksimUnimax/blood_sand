- the Institute of Russian Language RAS material discusses the Veles/Volos name problem and ancient source context;

But this does **not automatically prove** that a specific modern graphic marketed as `Печать Велеса` — bear paw, wolf paw, inverted-A-like sign, etc. — is itself an ancient attested emblem.

The bounded authority pass did not locate an equivalent primary/academic source establishing that exact current graphic chronology.

The gap is not “competitors know nothing about Veles.” The gap is **claim boundary discipline**:
- attested deity/history;
- later reconstruction;
- modern symbolic tradition;

must not be presented as one continuous ancient fact chain.

## 3. Vegvísir — strongest confirmed source-quality opportunity

### Current competitor claim

Ruyan's current article states that Vegvísir:
- is one of the most ancient Scandinavian symbols;
- helped Vikings conduct long sea voyages;
- belongs to galdrastafir which supposedly appeared in the early Middle Ages, `IV–VII centuries`.

### Checked manuscript/scholarly evidence

The checked manuscript chain identifies the Huld manuscript `ÍB 383 4to`, compiled by **Geir Vigfússon in Akureyri in 1860**, as containing the earliest known Vegvísir attestation in the checked record.

The manuscript is held by the National and University Library of Iceland; current manuscript/reference cataloguing points directly to Huld and its 1860 date.

A peer-reviewed 2025 paper by Roberto Luigi Pagani, published in `Gripla` and indexed by the University of Iceland, argues that Icelandic `galdrastafir` belong to a broader learned European magical/grimoire tradition, with strong medieval/Renaissance and Solomonic influence. It specifically cautions against automatically reading galdrastafir as ancient Norse runic designs.

### Decision

`SOURCE_RIGOR_GAP_VEGVISIR = VERY_HIGH / DIRECTLY DEMONSTRATED`.

This is stronger than a missing citation. There is an actual chronology conflict between a current specialist's Viking/IV–VII-century framing and the checked surviving evidence/scholarship.

Blood & Sand rule:
- do **not** call Vegvísir a Viking-Age symbol or Viking navigation instrument on current evidence;
- identify the documented Huld 1860 attestation;
- distinguish Icelandic galdrastafir from runes;
- if discussing “Viking compass” at all, label it as a modern popular association/misnomer, not historical fact;
- separate manuscript-stated magical purpose from experimentally verified navigation.

This creates a credible owned-content advantage for **OU07** because the underlying Yandex evidence already showed an explicit meaning/history job for Vegvísir.

## 4. Ægishjálmur / Шлем Ужаса

### Why this topic needs a two-layer explanation

The historical situation is different from Vegvísir.

The **word/concept `ægishjálmr` is genuinely medieval**. It occurs in Old Norse literary tradition such as `Fáfnismál` / the Fáfnir-Sigurðr complex and later saga material.

However, the familiar radial **drawn magical stave** belongs to the later Icelandic galdrastafir/manuscript tradition. Current scholarship on galdrastafir also points to European grimoire influence and warns against treating every stave as a Viking rune construction.

Current Ruyan commerce/category copy treats Ægishjálmur as an ancient Scandinavian magical symbol and adds battlefield/magical-effect claims without preserving that historical distinction.

### Decision

`SOURCE_RIGOR_GAP_AEGISHJALMUR = CONFIRMED`.

Blood & Sand should explain **two histories**:
1. medieval literary `helm of awe/terror` concept;
2. later drawn Icelandic magical stave.

Do not project the familiar radial drawing backward into Viking Age material without direct evidence.

This gives OU08 a more credible explanatory angle than generic “what the symbol protects from” copy.

## 5. Source-quality standard for Blood & Sand

### Tier A — direct/primary evidence

Preferred when available:
- manuscript scans/catalogue records;
- chronicles / medieval literary witnesses;
- recorded folklore text/corpus;
- museum/archive catalogue records.

Use for claims such as:
- `this form occurs in manuscript X`;
- `this word occurs in source Y`;
- `this folklore motif is recorded in collection Z`.

### Tier B — peer-reviewed / academic

Use for:
- chronology;
- source genealogy;
- contested interpretation;
- distinction between manuscript tradition and modern reconstruction.

Examples in the present pass:
- University of Iceland / `Gripla` research on galdrastafir;
- Institute of Russian Language RAS material for Veles/Volos source/etymology problems.

### Tier C — institutional/reference encyclopedia

Use for stable background facts when direct primary work is excessive for the user task.

### Tier D — commercial/popular/esoteric page

May be used only to report:
- modern interpretation;
- current belief/practice;
- market terminology;
- what sellers/users call the symbol.

It must **not** become authority for ancient chronology or product efficacy.

## 6. Required claim labels in future Blood & Sand content

Each potentially contested symbolic/history claim should fit one of:

- `ATTESTED` — directly recorded in a named primary/reference source;
- `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION` — supported by identifiable scholarship but not a direct primary statement;
- `MODERN_INTERPRETATION` — contemporary symbolic/esoteric reading;
- `SELLER_OR_PRACTITIONER_BELIEF` — belief/practice attributed to a group/source;
- `UNSUPPORTED_EFFECT_CLAIM` — efficacy not established; do not state as fact.

For client-facing prose these labels need not be shown as database enums, but the distinction must be obvious in ordinary Russian.

## 7. What Blood & Sand should explicitly avoid

Do not write as fact without direct evidence:
- `древний символ`, merely because a related deity/name is ancient;
- `этим пользовались викинги`, unless there is period evidence;
- `славяне наносили этот конкретный современный знак на...`, unless sourced;
- `защищает от аварий / сглаза / болезней / бед`, as objective efficacy;
- invented ancient dates for galdrastafir;
- modern product symbolism presented as continuous archaeological tradition.
